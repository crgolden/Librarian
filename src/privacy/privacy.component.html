Privacy Policy

Effective 2026-07-17. See the FAQ for a plainer walkthrough of linking your account.

What we collect

When you link your PlayStation account, Curator stores:

  • Your PlayStation account ID
  • An encrypted access token and refresh token, and when they expire
  • Your PlayStation library entitlements, and the derived, curated library built from them
  • Your consoles, install sizes, and any collections you create
  • If you choose to provide one, an encrypted RAWG and/or OpenCritic API key — entirely optional, used only to enrich your own library with genre/score metadata
  • A log of the high-level actions taken on your behalf (see "Your action history" below)
  • Any handle you choose to declare on another PlayStation site. Only the site and the handle are stored — the link itself is built from that site's own address template, and the list of sites you can pick from is fixed. Removing a handle deletes it.
  • If you've turned on trophy harvesting, one number per game in your library — how far through its trophy list you are — so you can filter and sort by it without Curator re-reading your whole library from PlayStation on every page load. Turning trophy harvesting back off erases those numbers, it doesn't just stop refreshing them.

What we never collect

  • Your NPSSO token — used once to obtain your access/refresh tokens, then discarded. It is never written to a database or a log line.
  • Your email address — compared in memory against PlayStation's verified email at link time, then forgotten. There is no email column anywhere in Curator's database.
  • Individual trophies — which ones you've earned, when, and how rare they are is read live from PlayStation and cached for 15 minutes, never stored permanently. Your per-game completion percentage is stored, and only if you've asked for it; see "What we collect" above.
  • Your presence, friends list, devices, or chat — read live if at all, never stored
  • Your real name, address, phone number, or any payment information

How it's protected

Access and refresh tokens are encrypted before they're stored; only Curator holds the key. If you provide a RAWG or OpenCritic key, it's encrypted with that same key. Every connection to Curator uses TLS. Your NPSSO is used once, in memory, and never persisted.

About RAWG/OpenCritic keys

Curator doesn't provision a shared RAWG or OpenCritic key for every user — it doesn't scale. If you choose to provide your own, it's stored encrypted and used only to enrich your own library; it's never shown back to you or anyone else after you save it, and you can remove it at any time from your PSN settings page.

If your key retrieves data Curator doesn't already have for a game (its genre, its critic score), that game metadata — never your key — is added to Curator's shared game catalog, so future lookups for that same game, yours or another user's, don't need to call RAWG/OpenCritic again. This is why some games in your library may already show enrichment data even if you've never provided a key yourself — someone else's key (or Curator's own periodic catalog refresh) already resolved it.

Sharing and visibility

Every collection you create starts private — visible only to you. From a collection's own page you can change that to unlisted (anyone with its link can open it — nobody discovers it any other way) or public (the same link, plus the collection is listed on your profile if your profile's own "show collections" setting is also on). Changing a collection back to private immediately breaks the old link; there's no separate "revoke" step, and no record kept of who opened it while it was shared.

A shared collection's link is the one part of Curator that doesn't require signing in at all — that's the point of a link you can hand to someone who's never used Curator. What it shows an anonymous visitor is exactly the collection's contents (titles, cover art, franchise, tier) and nothing else: not your account identity, your PlayStation ID, your library, or any other collection you own. A signed-in visitor can additionally follow a collection they don't own from its share page or your profile; unfollowing is always available, even for a collection that's since been made private again.

Handles you've declared on other PlayStation sites appear on your profile under the same rule as everything else there: a visitor sees them only once your profile is public. They're always visible to you, and you can remove one at any time from your profile settings.

Share links are kept out of search engines. Librarian publishes a sitemap so the public game catalog can be found on the web, and it lists only catalog pages — never a share link, a profile, a library, or a collection. Our robots.txt additionally asks crawlers to stay out of those areas entirely. An unlisted collection stays discoverable only by the link you hand out.

The public game catalog

The game catalog is public and needs no account to browse — it describes games, not people. It's built from the PlayStation Store's own public listings plus the titles that turn up in users' libraries, and a catalog page shows only facts about the game: its title, cover art, franchise, genre, tier, and review scores. It never names who owns a game, how many people own it, or whose library it came from.

One thing on a catalog page is yours alone: if you're signed in and you own the game, the page shows your trophy progress for it. That figure is only ever sent to you. Signed out, it isn't in the response at all — not hidden with CSS, not blanked out in the browser, simply absent from what the server sends. Nobody viewing the same page sees your progress, and you never see theirs.

Advertising and tracking

We don't run ads, and we never will. There is no ad network on this site, no ad-tracking or retargeting cookies, and no advertising or marketing company ever receives your data. The only monitoring in place is operational telemetry used to notice outages and errors, not to build a profile of you or sell one to anyone.

How long we keep it

Your library, tokens, and account data are kept until you unlink or delete your account. Trophy reads are cached for 15 minutes and expire on their own; your stored per-game completion percentages are kept alongside the rest of your library and are erased the moment you turn trophy harvesting off, unlink, or delete your account. Your action history (below) is kept for one year, including for a period after you delete your account — see "Your action history."

Your controls

From your PSN settings page you can, at any time:

  • Unlink — immediately deletes your stored PlayStation tokens
  • Delete my data — immediately deletes your account and everything associated with it: your PSN link, library, consoles, collections, any saved preferences, and any RAWG/OpenCritic keys. This is instant and self-service; you don't need to email anyone to make it happen.
  • Remove a RAWG/OpenCritic key — independent of the above; removes just that key, immediately, without touching anything else in your account.

Your action history

If your PlayStation account were ever compromised by something unrelated to Curator and you needed to know exactly what Curator itself did with your account, this is how we — and you — can check. Curator keeps a log of the high-level actions it takes on your behalf: linking attempts (and why one failed, if it did), unlinking, library refresh requests, trophy fetches, and account deletion. It never contains your NPSSO, your tokens, or raw data pulled from PlayStation — only which action happened and when.

You can view and download this history yourself at any time from your PSN settings page. Because its purpose is to answer disputes about what happened, this log is kept for one year and is not deleted immediately when you delete your account — it outlives the "Delete my data" action for that one-year window, then is purged automatically.

Open source

Every claim on this page is verifiable in public code — we'd rather you check than take our word for it. Librarian (this site and its BFF) is at github.com/crgolden/Librarian. Curator (the API that handles your NPSSO, verifies your email, and stores your data) is at github.com/crgolden/Curator. A few specific places worth pointing to directly: token encryption lives in persistence/crypto.py, linking (including the never-persisted-NPSSO / in-memory email match) is in link_service.py, the no-email-column schema tenet is documented in db/migrations/0001_initial.sql, the action-history log described above is db/migrations/0003_account_action_log.sql, and the RAWG/OpenCritic key storage/routes described above are db/migrations/0004_user_enrichment_keys.sql and enrichment_keys_routes.py.

Both projects' static-analysis results are public too, not just the source that produced them. Librarian's scan is at sonarcloud.io/project/overview?id=crgolden_Librarian, Curator's is at sonarcloud.io/project/overview?id=crgolden_Curator. Anyone can see the same open bugs, coverage gaps, and duplicated code we do — nothing about the quality of either codebase is cleaned up before you look.
